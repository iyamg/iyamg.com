[SoCraTes](https://socrates-conference.at/) is built on a simple observation, that typically, a conference experience is defined by how much you enjoyed the coffee break conversations. 

I had heard about the conference a few times, and every time someone would mention it, they were kind of obsessed. You know when someone enjoyed something so much, it's annoying you, because you're jealous. Plus, I don't like having my expectations raised too high.

### Marketplace: the pitches you never get to hear
If you go to the conference website even 5 minutes before it starts, there's no schedule, because it doesn't exist. I had been told several times that the agenda is set collectively, and kept wondering - what is that supposed to mean? 

Basically, the day opens with a wide board of room/time placeholders. Everyone lines up and pitches the sessions. Sessions, not _talks_, because in a way, it's not just the presenters pitching, but the audience as well. A de facto approach for talks is: "I am sharing my expertise/experience", but who decided that? During the opening session, [Pierluigi Pugliese](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ppugliese/) reminded us to be more open-minded, because there are two other ways to connect with others:

* *I want to learn this, come help me learn*
* *Let's create something together*

I won't go on describing the experience for too long, because, first of all, you really should've been there™️, and second, I'd rather give you a minimal snapshot of the experience:
### The sessions I decided to join
* Software organism - unexplored interplay of OpenTelemetry and OpenFeature
* Modularizing the monolith - sharing approaches, strategies, experience. Found out about enforceable architectures
* Measuring performance in the engineering team - discussion prompt from a co-founder of a scale-up, whose investors just found out what a PR is and asked him to start tracking produced LOC
* General GitOps discussion
* AI writes code / How to protect knowledge - this was a "what do we do" session.
* Let's talk Data Mesh (this was me yay)
* Centralize the loop - how to get our development environment off our laptops (my unrealized dream)
* Pub Quiz
* Laya prompting - Someone wanted to try out this open-source  system one model (like jev). It was quite fun to go to the landing page as a group and try to navigate it

### A few I wanted to join but couldn't
* Hiring in the age of Agents - designing technical interviews for testing ai skills 
* A few other GitOps sessions... One of them was about doing GitOps and RG deployments with docker-compose...
* Creating programmatic audio-visual experiences using llms
* Something about cool vintage tech
* Zen and the art of Common Lisp 
* Architectural snapshot testing
* Walk and talk
* PowerPoint karaoke

### The anywhere/anytime category
* "I brought a card game about designing CI pipelines; it will be at the front desk"
* "I just finished a seven-year DnD campaign"
* "Let's play Hybrid digital/physical game about pushing" (I was as confused as you are)
* "I brought whiskey"
* "I am writing a book"

Also, two unusual topics. They are very related.
* How to avoid AI in modern Software Development
* How to choose a new career if you want to leave Software Development

##  I'll wrap up with 5 principles of Open Space:

* Whenever it starts is the right time
* Wherever it is is the right place
* Whoever comes is the right people
* When it's over, it's over
* Whatever happens is the only thing that could have

These posters were on the wall in the main room, but I don't think they're only about the conference.
